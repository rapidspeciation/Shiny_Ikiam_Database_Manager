# Product capability catalog

This is the full set of useful capabilities to consider for an Ithomiini field, insectary, specimen, and research data system. It is a product vision, not a promise that every feature exists or belongs in the first release. The [25 September 2026 workbook profile](database-findings.md), [sheet headers](workbook-schema.json), [meeting review](meetings.md), [original request and app review](discovery.md), and [workflow notes](workflows.md) supply the evidence. Meeting notes describe practice and problems, not verified completeness of historical rows. The present application has not been implemented.

In the catalog, **sheet-backed** means the current workbook has named fields or tables for the information. It does not mean the proposed screen or process exists. **Add** means the product needs a new event, link, field, rule, or service to deliver the action. **Verify** means staff must settle the meaning, method, or authority before it can be treated as a fact. Existing source values and formulas should remain visible when a new model adds detail.

## Collect and follow individuals

| Capability and example action | Current evidence | Product additions or decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Field capture, sampling, and recapture. "Start a transect visit, record a marked butterfly, then log its later recapture." | `Collection_data` has release/collection, field mark, insectary and CAM IDs, taxonomy, sex, collector, location, transect, bait, stratum, date/time, weather, flight height, weight, notes, death and preservation fields. [Workflows](workflows.md) describes preserved, marked/released, released/unmarked, and sent-to-insectary choices. `SamplingDay_data` has visit time, place, purpose, collectors, logger, and notes. Historical documentation mentions mark reuse but current headers differ. | Action-specific forms and required fields; explicit session defaults for collector, place, weather, and date; ten newest entries and configurable Load more. Link capture and recapture encounters to the same verified field mark and visit, with disposition and sampling effort. Keep the original prompt's FS/FF tube choices where relevant. Verify mark reuse, allowed values, and effort denominators before deriving capture or survival rates. |
| 2. Identity, lookup, and individual history. "Scan N4D or a CAM ID, choose the correct match, and see species, sex, clutch, parents, treatments, photos, and tubes." | Workbook IDs include field marks, insectary IDs, CAM IDs, tubes, sample IDs, and external IDs. The historical insectary sequence reached `9ZZ`, then assigned records use letter-digit-letter from `A0A`. Repeated marks and CAM/photo discrepancies appear in [findings](database-findings.md) and [meetings](meetings.md). Collection and insectary species conventions differ. | Allocate each ID under its own current policy, accounting for preallocated rows and concurrent saves. Search all identifiers, display every match with sheet, row, dates and origin, and offer human-reviewed alias/duplicate reconciliation. Build a sourced individual timeline and compact phone card. Show missing or conflicting events; never treat a bare mark as globally unique or preservation condition as current life status. |
| 3. Death, disappearance, preservation, and correction. "Record that this individual disappeared on Tuesday; later record a confirmed death and ethanol preservation." | Main sheets have death and preservation dates, cause/medium, condition, tissue location, and notes. Some `Collection_data` values come from `Insectary_data` formulas. Meetings distinguish death, later preservation, and research eligibility. | Record observed death, last seen/disappearance, inferred status, preservation event, and destination separately. Support corrected dates and reasons with history. Write to the source record and preserve linked formulas; show contradictions rather than counting all `Alive` preservation values as living butterflies. |

## Manage cages, development, and breeding

| Capability and example action | Current evidence | Product additions or decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4. Clutches and daily stage rounds. "At cage 12, count larvae and pupae for clutch 944, then see the last count." | `Insectary_stocks` records eggs, hatch, larvae, pupae, earliest emergence, and adults; `Insectary_data` links individuals by `CLUTCH NUMBER`. [Meeting 79](meetings.md) says the cage notebook gave staff an immediate view before transfer. | Dated, attributed count observations per clutch and stage, including corrections, confidence, and notes. Show the latest round, trend, stage transitions, and first emergence at the cage. Define whether a count is total, newly changed, or removed before computing change. Keep workbook summary formulas intact. |
| 5. Cage, clutch, and emergence operations. "Move clutch 944 to cage 12, see the current occupants, then add six emerged adults." | `Insectary_stocks` records stock, stage counts and dates; `Insectary_data` links individuals to clutches and records sex, origin, and life stage. `Hybrid_Attempts` has `Jail_N` and return-to-stock dates, but the headers do not establish a general movement history. Stock totals can be formula-derived. | Add cage identity, introductions, relocations, removals, returns, and current/past occupancy. Batch adult entry from a clutch with per-individual sex and emergence date, unique current-format ID allocation, and stock-count preview. Distinguish unused prefilled ID rows from butterflies; allow unknown sex and later correction. Verify cage naming and occupancy rules. |
| 6. Mating attempts and crosses. "Pair these two individuals, record courtship today and observed mating tomorrow." | `Melinaea_crosses`, `Stocks_Matings`, `Crosses_Lys_x_Pol`, and `Hybrid_Attempts` have different pairing, treatment, cage, mating, and finish fields. Meetings distinguish attempted pairings from confirmed mating. | One linked attempt history that retains each study's fields and source tab. Show partner sex, species/form, age, origin, condition, and prior attempts; record introduction, checks, observed courtship/mating, separation, and reason. Do not infer mating from eggs or a pair sharing a cage. |
| 7. Eggs, offspring, and pedigrees. "Record eggs laid by this pair, fungus damage, preserved eggs, hatch, and the resulting clutch." | `Melinaea_eggs` has parents, clutch, eggs, preservation reason/condition, tube, and rack. `Insectary_stocks`, `Melinaea_crosses`, and `F1/F2_MutationRate` carry counts, generations, ancestors, and sample IDs. The [meetings](meetings.md) describe daily egg charts and F1/F2 summary dependencies. | Link dated egg batches, outcomes, preserved subsets, clutches, offspring, parents, and grandparents. Keep biological observation separate from inferred fertilization. Reconcile cross, stock, individual, and F1/F2 tables before showing a derived family total. Flag missing parent specimens and unresolved pedigree links. |
| 8. Research eligibility and mutation summaries. "List offspring eligible for the mutation-rate family and explain each exclusion." | `Research_purpose`, pedigree, preservation, and `F1/F2_MutationRate` fields exist. [Meeting 62](meetings.md) says some F1s were ineligible because parents were not preserved; [Meeting 137](meetings.md) describes existing pivot dependencies. | Make eligibility criteria and exclusions versioned, reviewable study rules, not a universal biological fact. Show numerator, denominator, source rows, missing data, and the exact method used for each mutation or hatch metric. Verify protocol definitions and how genotyping evidence changes status before presenting a rate. |

## Delivery and evidence boundaries

The interface can be served from GitHub Pages. A separate authenticated backend on the existing `claudeclaw` host should own data access, ID allocation, validation, audit, synchronization, and AI calls; private Google and AI credentials stay on that backend. [Architecture](architecture.md) owns the detailed design. This catalog does not make Apps Script the primary backend. During the trial, writes target the personal test copy. Formula-bearing cells require operation-specific write rules.

Shared usability and operations also include Spanish/English labels where needed, accessible controls, large phone targets, keyboard/scanner support, consistent desktop views, contextual protocol help, and printable field or cage summaries. Imports should preview validation and duplicate candidates before saving. Exports should preserve IDs, units, dates, and data provenance. Backups, tested restoration, service rollback, and explicit sandbox/production indicators are part of operating the application.

Delivery can proceed in stages without shrinking the product vision: first identity, lookup, field/insectary entry, current clutch view, death/preservation, and safe saves; then linked breeding and sample workflows; then inventory, specialist experiments, reporting, and reviewed AI assistance. Cross-cutting audit, access control, source links, and correction paths begin with the first write, not as a final stage. Each stage needs field-user validation against a real notebook round and the sandbox workbook before production use.

The user has chosen routine editing through both the app and Sheets. The [history and selected undo proposal](edit-history.md) combines exact app changes with observed external changes and makes gaps in native edit evidence explicit.

The decisions that matter most before implementation are the current ID allocation and reuse rules, the source of truth for each editable field, the definitions of cage occupancy and life status, research eligibility and rate methods, treatment and specimen protocols, the direct-edit observation mechanism, roles, and offline requirements. These are verification tasks, not reasons to omit the capabilities above.
