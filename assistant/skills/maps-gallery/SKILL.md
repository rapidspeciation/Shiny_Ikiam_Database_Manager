# Maps, host plants and the Wings Gallery

Nine read-only tools; none changes the workbook. Use them before any outside API: they hold the maps' butterfly records, host plants, the plants' GBIF records and photos, and build the map links. Call GBIF, iNaturalist or other sites only for what they do not have, and say so.

| Question | Tool |
|---|---|
| Where are the best places to find X (in a country, near a place, between two elevations)? | `maps_localities` |
| The same with a map to send or show (where to collect males of X and Y?), and any of these questions on WhatsApp | `maps_snapshot` |
| What has been recorded around a place, or above some elevation in a region? | `maps_species` |
| What plants does X use? Which Ithomiini are on this plant genus? | `host_plants` |
| Which host plants of X would we find in a province or canton of Ecuador (Napo, cantón Tena)? Photos of them | `host_plants` with `area` {province: "Napo"} (or {canton}) and `photo_sheet: true`; look at the sheets, then `send_sheet_photos` |
| Which of X's host plants grow in Ecuador (near Tena, in a box)? Where to collect them, a map of their records | `host_plants` with `area`, `country`, `near` or `bbox` (one call) |
| Near Ikiam (or another point), which site should I go to first to collect X's host plants? Where around here? | `host_plant_sites` with `near` {lat, lng, km} (Ikiam is about -0.95, -77.86); `rank: "grid"` for cells instead of the project's localities |
| Our collection's specimens: CAM IDs, a locality, "nuestros especímenes", the Wings Gallery | `gallery_specimens` |
| Photos of the eggs, larvae or pupae of X | `taxon_photos` with `life_stage` (the project's clutch photos, iNaturalist's Life Stage annotation, GBIF's lifeStage); look at the sheets, then `send_sheet_photos` |
| Photos of X, of its adults, females or males (in an area) | `taxon_photos` with `life_stage: "adult"`, `sex`, `area` (also the Wings Atlas records' photos); look at the sheets, then `send_sheet_photos` |
| Photos of X alive, in the field («viva», «en el campo») | `taxon_photos` with `life_stage: "adult"` and `living: true`: no preserved specimens; iNaturalist photos without a stage annotation are captioned so |
| Photos of one part of a host plant: the underside of the leaves (el envés), a fruit, the stem or latex | `host_plants` with each `plant` alone (for X's host plants, one call per plant), `country: ["Ecuador"]`, `photo_sheet: true`, `later_photos: true` (the records' extra photos, where observers put undersides and close-ups); `photo_skip: 18` for the next ones. Look, pick only tiles that show that part, say how many photos you looked at |
| Photos of X laying eggs, or on its host plant | `taxon_photos` with `life_stage: ["egg", "adult"]` **and** `host_plants` with the named `area`, else `country: "Ecuador"`, and `photo_sheet: true`; see "Egg-laying" below |

## Answering

- **Where to find a species.** Give the first few sites with their records, years seen (a site with records over many years is a better bet than one busy day) and elevation, and each site's map link. Say which sources the records come from: the project's own collection (Sanger Institute), museum records (GBIF), Dore et al. (2022), iNaturalist. Dore et al. records are 0.25° grid cells (about 28 km): say where the species occurs from them, never a site to visit. End with the link to the map with all of them and the data's date (`freshness`). On WhatsApp the answer carries the map as a picture: use `maps_snapshot` (it ranks the sites the same way) and end with its `images_block`; its `map_url` may go in the text before it. A maps link alone is not a map there.
- **One sex.** Give `sex`. The project's records nearly all have it, museum (GBIF) records about four in ten, iNaturalist and Dore et al. almost never; so the ranking counts that sex's records, and each site's `by_sex` (and `own_by_sex`, the project's) says how many males, females and unknown it has. Say this when the sources lack it.
- **A map picture.** `maps_snapshot` gives `image` with `caption` and `images_block` (on WhatsApp end the answer with `images_block` as it is: that sends the picture), `map_url` to open the same view live, `picture_labels` (how each site appears on the picture), `elsewhere` (good sites outside the picture) and `best_by_taxon` (each taxon's own best sites, for one the shared ranking puts low). Give `country` when the person means their own region. Leave `frame` out for "where to collect" (the best site and the others within 150 km); `frame: "all"` only when the person asks for the whole range.
  - `renderer: "app"`: the maps app's own picture (its title, legend with an "Unknown subspecies" row, site labels, scale bar). Name the sites as `picture_labels[].label_on_picture` shows them (a label may merge nearby sites: "X +2 sites"); there are no numbers on it. The legend counts all the records asked (`caption`: N records at M sites), not only the `records.on_picture` visible in the picture: say both when they differ. Sites in `hidden_sites` are inside the frame but covered by the legend or title: do not say they are shown.
  - `renderer: "pillow"`: a simpler picture (OpenTopoMap relief, dots by species, numbered sites: `on_map`, also `label_on_picture`). `degraded.not_kept` lists what it lacks: never describe the app's basemap, colours, site labels or legend for it. Do not explain `degraded.reason` unless asked; a filter the app lacks (`area`, elevation, `years`, `near`) always gives this picture.
- **Elevation.** The project's records use the workbook's ELEVATION; the others the ground's elevation at their coordinates (a 90 m DEM). Say so when an elevation band was asked, and how many records had no elevation.
- **Host plants.** Give each plant with its confidence and its citation and link. Catalogue rows (Beccaloni et al. 2008) are published records, often from far away: not evidence that the butterfly uses that plant at a given place. A genus- or family-level host means the plant species is unknown.
- **Host plants in an area.** One `host_plants` call with the area gives each plant's records there, years and top sites (coordinates and the nearest named collecting place), the best sites for all of them, photo thumbnails (`markdown`) and `map_url`: the Wings Atlas with the host-plant layer, those plants and the country on. Name the plants with no records there too. The records are the maps' GBIF download (its date in `records_note`), so they can be fewer than GBIF shows today. Always say that a plant growing somewhere is not evidence that the butterfly uses it there. Genus-level hosts are counted but left off the sites and map: their records are of any species of the genus.
- **A province or canton.** Every tool with a region takes `area`: {province: "Napo"} or {canton: "Tena"}, by INEC's outline, not a box. Use it whenever the person names one.
- **Host plants in a province or canton.** `host_plants` with `area` gives each plant's `status`: `in_area` (records inside, with years and counts), `nearby` (none inside, some within `nearby_km`, default 50 km: counts, years, provinces and the nearest record) or `far`; `in_area`, `nearby_only` and `not_near` list them. Answer in that order and keep the line clear: plants with records in the area are recorded there; plants only nearby are candidates, likely but not recorded there (give their nearest record); the rest are hosts elsewhere. Few records (one or two, or decades old) are a weak lead: say so.
- **Photos to send: look first, then pick.** Ask for `photo_sheet: true` (host_plants with an area, or gallery_specimens): you get 1–2 contact sheets (`photo_sheets`, numbered 3×3 tiles, each with the plant and where the photo was taken). Read each sheet's `path` to see it, then choose for every plant a tile that shows its leaves or the whole plant (how it is found in the field; a nearer photo only when it shows them as well), and after those one flower where there is one; at most 5. A plant whose tiles show no leaves or whole plant: say that none of its photos on the sheet shows them (a sheet of that plant alone, `plant` with `photo_sheet`, shows more). `send_photos` gives the same sheets. Call `send_sheet_photos` with the sheets' ids, those `tiles` and `lang: "es"` for Spanish: its `images` are files of about 2048 px whose captions say recorded or CANDIDATE and credit the photographer and licence; on WhatsApp end the reply with its `images_block` as it is.
- **Butterfly photos (`taxon_photos`).** Adults come also from the Wings Atlas records (the project's specimens, museum and iNaturalist records in the maps), marked "Atlas" on the tiles: no life stage or behaviour is recorded on them, so check the tile shows an adult. `sex` keeps adults recorded as that sex only (most iNaturalist photos have none: say so). A source in `sources_not_checked` (or a stage in `not_checked`) did not answer even after a second try: say the search was incomplete, never that there are none. Only `none_for` means none.
- **Egg-laying ("¿hay fotos de X poniendo huevos?").** No source records the behaviour. Call `taxon_photos` (egg and adult) and `host_plants` with the area named, else `country: "Ecuador"`, and `photo_sheet: true`. Answer: egg photos if any (say plainly when none, or when a source was not checked); adult photos as adults; host-plant photos as the plants where the eggs may be laid, labelled as context. Never say or imply a host-plant or adult photo shows oviposition.
- **When you cannot look at a sheet.** If `photo_sheet_error` comes back (or you cannot read images), `host_plants` again with `send_photos` and `tags_fallback: true` gives photos picked by the photo tags, nobody having looked; each caption says "elegida automáticamente por etiqueta, no revisada". Send them with `images_block` and say in the answer that they were chosen automatically and not checked.
- **Which site first.** `host_plant_sites` ranks the project's collection localities (or 1–2 km cells) by the host plants' records around them, nearer records weighing more. For each site you recommend give its score with the records behind it (within 2, 5 and 10 km), the nearest record (plant, distance, year), the oldest and newest years, its elevation and the project's records of the butterfly there; then its photo thumbnails (`markdown`) and the `map_url`. Always say: GBIF and iNaturalist records follow roads, stations and observers, so a high score can mean a well-sampled place rather than more plants; records from decades ago may be gone; and a plant record is not evidence that the butterfly uses the plant there. Few records behind a score (one or two) is a weak lead: say so.
- **Photos.** Put the tool's `markdown` in the reply as it is: the chat shows those thumbnails, each linking to its full photo, with the credit line. Add the gallery link for the rest. A specimen added to the workbook since the gallery's last update is in the answer but not yet on the gallery site (`update_websites` brings it).
- **Showing them.** On this server `html_preview` does not work (its browser is blocked): never use it. The `markdown` thumbnails in the reply are the way to show photos; `html_render` works if a laid-out page is really needed.
- Names: a species is "Genus species"; a subspecies "Genus species subspecies". A name the maps do not know comes back in `not_in_maps`: check the spelling with the person, or the workbook's Taxonomy sheet.
